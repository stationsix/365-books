# Batch 1 verification notes, ranks 11 to 13 (2026-11-26)

Three separate review agents, one per book; none saw the drafting or the drafters' notes. About 140 factual claims checked. All three rated high confidence and set to verified.

The drafting agents in this run could search but not open pages, so the drafts were written from Claude's knowledge of each book. The reviewers were told to treat every claim as unchecked and confirm it from pages they actually read, cutting or softening anything they could not. Every claim in the final text rests on the sources listed below and in each file's front matter.

## Fixes applied
- The End of Competitive Advantage (about 50 claims): an unconfirmed New York City government role replaced with McGrath's published bio (IT director, politics, two start-ups); "since the early 1990s" now "since her 1993 Wharton PhD"; the 1995 HBR article with Ian C. MacMillan added. An unsourced line about which people suit each stage of the wave cut; the book's own framing (equal emphasis across the life cycle, moving talent between stages) used instead. Disengagement rewritten to match the book's Table 3-1 and the exit types in Table 3-2. Stability now uses the book's elements plus the growth-outlier screen figures (4,793 firms over $1B, at least 5% net income growth each year 2000 to 2009, ten qualified) and Infosys reorganizing every two to three years. Arena definition set to customer segment, offer and location, with the chess versus Go image (Table 1-1). An unconfirmed "hostage" phrase dropped; owning versus accessing assets added. An unsourced claim that some outliers later slowed cut; critiques from George S. Day and Mind Tools added, the rest labeled as our caveats. Trimmed to about 1,560 words.
- The Art of Action (about 45 claims): bio now 17 years at BCG in London and Munich, plus *Alamein*. An invented tone line replaced with the publisher's note on the cases (pharmaceuticals to Formula 1). Effects gap reworded to the book's "hope" rather than "expect"; friction "reduced but never eliminated". Mission command now says each level works out what it will do; the command maxim credited to Moltke. Directed opportunism rewritten around alignment on intent and autonomy on actions. Briefing contents matched to the book (intent up to two levels above, implied tasks, main effort, freedoms and constraints); an unsourced "dialogue" line cut. The book's definition of strategy quoted (the only quote). Drafted critiques replaced with points from David Hurst (*strategy+business*) and Neville Parton (*Air Power Review*); two remaining points labeled as our caveats.
- Platform Revolution (about 47 claims): bios corrected (Van Alstyne professor of information systems at Questrom; MIT IDE "affiliated"; Choudary's firm is Platform Thinking Labs). Android cut from the iPhone example. A "customer value to ecosystem value" shift cut: it likely comes from the authors' 2016 HBR article, not the book. Monetization narrowed to what excerpts show (capturing a portion of the excess value created, four techniques, subsidizing one side). Openness and governance matched to the book (three governance rules; laws, norms, architecture and markets as tools). A claim that the panel chose this book over *Reshuffle* corrected to match the batch file. Kirkus review added (the only quote). Sources the text does not rely on removed. About 1,565 words.

## Process notes for future batches
- When drafters cannot open pages, say so in the reviewer brief and have the reviewer treat the whole draft as unchecked. It worked here, but the reviewers did far more rewriting than usual.
- Drafters invent plausible lines about a book's tone and about the site's own selection debates. Check both against the publisher page and the batch file.
- Audiobook companion PDFs again settled which tables and examples are really in the book.
