# Batch 1 verification notes, ranks 17 to 19 (2026-11-26)

Three separate review agents, one per book. None saw the drafting or the drafters' notes. About 185 factual claims checked. All three rated high confidence and set to verified.

## Fixes applied
- Competitive Advantage (about 70 claims): structure rebuilt from the full 15-chapter table of contents. Choosing competitors moved to Part I (chapter 6), where the draft had placed it under competitive scope. Value chain wording set to the book's section headings ("linkages within the chain", "vertical linkages"). Cost drivers: an unsourced "drop activities" line cut, and discretionary policies named as the driver most in management's hands. The four tests for a worthwhile technological change rested on one commercial summary and were cut; the technology concept now rests on the table of contents and the abstract of Porter's 1985 *Journal of Business Strategy* article. Good-competitor benefits follow the table of contents. Uniqueness drivers and segmentation variables softened to "such as" lists. "Fit between activities" cut from the lens, since it is the 1996 "What Is Strategy?" idea, not this book's. Aktouf's critique quoted accurately ("tautological"); "long and dense" labeled as our caveat. No quotes used.
- The Innovation Stack (about 55 claims): both quotes turned out to come from a 2021 Cresset interview, not the book. "Copy when you can; invent when you must" is kept and credited to the interview; the other became a paraphrase and was removed from a prompt. McKelvey's St. Louis Fed title set to deputy chair of the board (effective 1 January 2020, before the March 2020 release). "What makes a problem perfect is the person" framed as a later interview remark. The walled-city image specified as medieval Edinburgh. Amazon Local Register details dated (introductory 1.75% rate, shut down October 2015, support ended 1 February 2016). Southwest and Bank of Italy stacks aligned to published lists. An unsupported claim that every attacked company ignored its attacker cut, as was a founder-traits list. Survivorship critique labeled as our caveat.
- The Cold Start Problem (about 60 claims): interview count changed from TechCrunch's "around 30" to Chen's own "more than a hundred". Publisher set to Harper Business. Wikipedia figure set to about 4,000 volunteers making more than 100 edits a month; Uber power drivers to "20% of drivers but 60% of trips", from Chen's own chapter post. Gmail, Facebook and Dropbox examples cut (no source). Uber's "$50 million a week" incentive figure cut (single source). Unconfirmed ceiling causes (churn, fraud) cut; Usenet's Eternal September added from book highlights. A "parity" claim about moats replaced with Chen's doubt that winner takes all.

## Confirmed and worth noting

Nine value chain activities and ten cost drivers each from two or more sources (Cambridge IfM, MIT OpenCourseWare slides citing chapter 3). Nothing from *Competitive Strategy* (1980) is presented as new here.

The lost American Express sale, co-founding Square with Jack Dorsey, and Amazon's retreat confirmed by the publisher. Shake Shack, Steak 'n Shake, Ted Drewes and the Wright brothers confirmed as book examples by summaries.

Slack "under ten" and the Caltrain station launch from Chen's excerpt on Lenny's Newsletter. Fresno BankAmericard mailing (60,000 cards, 1958) from a16z.

## Process notes for future batches
- Check that any quote attributed to a book actually appears in it. Both Innovation Stack quotes came from an interview.
- A retailer's full table of contents with section headings is a good second source to check commercial summaries against.
- Web fetch summaries sometimes echo back the terms asked about when the page lacks them. Rely only on results that quote the page directly.
- Unauthorized full-text PDFs turned up for two of the three books; reviewers avoided them.
