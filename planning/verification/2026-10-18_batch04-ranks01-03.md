# Batch 4 verification notes, ranks 1 to 3 (2026-10-18)

Three separate review agents, one per book; none saw the drafting. A fourth checked the three Batch 4 honorable mention cards. About 90 factual claims checked across the summaries. High Output Management and The Fearless Organization rated high confidence. Radical Candor was rated medium by its reviewer because two publisher pages blocked automated fetching and several critiques were unsourced; those critiques were cut or softened, so it is published at high confidence on the strength of the confirmed core facts.

## Fixes applied
- High Output Management (32 claims): "speed with error rate" was not Grove's paired-indicator example; replaced with inventory levels and shortages. Two AI-lens lines softened so they no longer say what Grove "valued most" or ranked highest (training is the activity he does name as high leverage). Dead intel.com source replaced with Intel's 2016 press release.
- The Fearless Organization (30 claims): the "punished or humiliated" definition comes from Edmondson's 2019 interviews, not the book; replaced with the book's wording ("the belief that the work environment is safe for interpersonal risk taking") and the interview wording kept as an unquoted gloss. Hospital study wording corrected to "better teams." Learning zone label matched to the book's figure ("learning and high-performance zone"). Concept 7 softened to what the book's survey-measures appendix supports. Two source URLs replaced (Wiley fetch blocked; Google page redirects).
- Radical Candor (28 claims): Scott's go-to question asks how to make it easier to work with "me," not "you." Revised-edition claim narrowed to a new preface and afterword addressing misreadings. Critiques about gender and about applying the advice upward softened or cut (no source). Career line tightened (she led AdSense, YouTube and DoubleClick teams). Macmillan source replaced with Wikipedia.
- Honorable mentions: Execution card now describes Bossidy as former chief executive of AlliedSignal and Honeywell.

## Confirmed and worth noting
- Grove joined Intel in 1968, president 1979, CEO 1987; book 1983, Vintage edition 1995. Output formula wording matches common citations; HOM uses Maslow's hierarchy. Doerr learned objectives and key results in Grove's Intel course in 1975.
- Edmondson: 1999 Administrative Science Quarterly paper (vol. 44, no. 2); the book covers Volkswagen, Wells Fargo, Columbia (Rodney Rocha) and Project Aristotle; leader's toolkit matches Table 7.1.
- Scott: the "Bob" story (ten months without criticism, then fired) is in the 2015 First Round article. Scott has said people use Radical Candor "as an excuse to act like a garden variety jerk" (Bain podcast).

## Process notes for future batches
- Famous definitions often come from an author's interviews or later articles, not the book itself. Check which source a quoted definition comes from before attributing it to the book.
- Examples that "sound like" the author (a paired indicator, a go-to question) are where drafting drifts; verify each one quoted or attributed.
