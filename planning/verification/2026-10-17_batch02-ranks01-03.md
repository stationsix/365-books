# Batch 2 verification notes, ranks 1 to 3 (2026-10-17)

Three separate review agents, one per book; none saw the drafting. About 104 factual claims checked. All three rated high confidence.

## Fixes applied
- The Innovator's Dilemma (about 30 claims): no factual errors. "Young" HBS professor changed to "relatively new" (he joined the faculty in 1992, about age 40) and his background stated as consultant and co-founder of a materials manufacturer (CPS Technologies). iPhone critique made checkable: he predicted in 2007 that it would not succeed. "Most quoted insight" softened to "a central insight."
- The Mythical Man-Month (about 34 claims): Brooks founded UNC's computer science department in 1964, during OS/360, not "later." The 1995 edition adds four chapters (No Silver Bullet, "No Silver Bullet" Refired, Propositions, After 20 Years), not two. Chapter-title description changed from "folklore" to "proverbs." Unsupported open-source claim in the critiques replaced with Brooks calling the law an oversimplification and studies showing it bites mainly on tightly sequential tasks. Unreachable Kartick source replaced.
- The Design of Everyday Things (about 40 claims): no factual errors. Apple role named precisely (Apple Fellow, then User Experience Architect). Reason for adding signifiers reworded to match the 2013 preface. Error remedies no longer imply Norman endorses confirmation dialogs.

## Confirmed and worth noting
- Brooks's law quote is exact: "Adding manpower to a late software project makes it later." (Ch. 2).
- King and Baatartogtokh (MIT Sloan Management Review, 2015): 7 of 77 claimed disruptions fit all four elements of the theory.
- Christensen et al., "What Is Disruptive Innovation?" (HBR, 2015) objects to the loose usage of "disruptive."
- Brooks, Ch. 19: "Parnas Was Right, and I Was Wrong about Information Hiding."
- The Design of Everyday Things first published 1988 by Basic Books as The Psychology of Everyday Things; retitled 1990; 2013 edition adds Ch. 6 and 7.

## Open items
- Library of Congress records not reached for The Innovator's Dilemma (1997) or the 1975 first edition of The Mythical Man-Month; public library catalogs and Wikipedia agree on both years. Pull the LoC records when convenient.

## Process notes for future batches
- Biography lines are where small errors creep in (age, timing of career moves). Give the drafter dated facts, not adjectives.
- Count added chapters in anniversary editions from the publisher's table of contents.
- Critiques sections need a source for every empirical claim, same as the concepts.
