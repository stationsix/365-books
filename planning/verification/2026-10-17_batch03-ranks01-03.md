# Batch 3 verification notes, ranks 1 to 3 (2026-10-17)

Three separate review agents, one per book; none saw the drafting. About 96 factual claims checked. Mindset and Flow rated high confidence; Man's Search for Meaning rated medium.

## Fixes applied
- Mindset (24 claims): no errors of substance. "False growth mindset" is a section in Chapter 7 of the 2016 edition, not a chapter. Organizational research wording matched to Dweck's ("kept secrets, cut corners and cheated"; employees in growth cultures felt more empowered and supported in taking risks). Critiques now include the 2023 Macnamara and Burgoyne meta-analysis (little to no effect after accounting for study quality) alongside Yeager et al. 2019 (small gains for lower-achieving students). Sisk subgroup benefits softened to "tentative."
- Flow (38 claims): no reversals. Psychic entropy redefined as disorder in consciousness from information that conflicts with goals. Interview groups corrected (climbers, chess players, surgeons, dancers came from the 1975 studies; workers from experience sampling). "Fewer flow experiences than people expected" cut to "few." Challenge-skill critique now credits his collaborators (Massimini and Carli) with the refined models. "The answer surprised him" cut. Weak SuperSummary source replaced.
- Man's Search for Meaning (34 claims): no factual errors. Nietzsche quote corrected to the Beacon wording ("can bear with almost any how"). The 1946 German book was the camp account only; the logotherapy part came with later English editions. Existential vacuum wording corrected (no instinct says what one has to do, no tradition what one ought to do). Removed a line echoing "between stimulus and response there is a space," which is misattributed to Frankl. Manuscript softened to "one of his reasons." Christmas 1944 anecdote made specific, attributed to "a camp doctor." Success-as-by-product located in the 1984 preface. Critiques add Frankl's own point that the best prisoners did not return (paraphrased to stay within two quotes).

## Confirmed and worth noting
- Mueller and Dweck (1998): intelligence-praised children chose easy tasks (67% vs 8%, Study 1) and misreported scores more often (38% vs 13%).
- Sisk et al. (2018): r = .10 between mindset and achievement; intervention d = 0.08.
- Satya Nadella credited Mindset with the "intuition" for Microsoft's culture change (2017).
- Csikszentmihalyi and LeFevre (1989): workers reported more flow at work, more motivation in leisure.
- Frankl: deported September 1942 to Theresienstadt, then Auschwitz, Kaufering and Turkheim (Dachau subcamps). Father died at Theresienstadt; mother and brother Walter murdered at Auschwitz; wife Tilly died after transfer to Bergen-Belsen.
