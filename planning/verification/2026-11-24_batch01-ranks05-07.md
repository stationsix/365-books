# Batch 1 verification notes, ranks 5 to 7 (2026-11-24)

Three separate review agents, one per book; none saw the drafting. About 135 factual claims checked. All three rated high confidence and set to verified.

Unbundling credited to Hagel and Singer, as the book does; a drafter's own grouping of the patterns cut. - Blue Ocean Strategy (about 45 claims): Bratton's subway rides moved to his Transit Police years, kept apart from his NYPD results (commissioner from February 1994, felony crime down 39% in under two years, no budget increase, as the authors report). Utility levers corrected to the book's six (customer productivity, simplicity, convenience, risk, fun and image, environmental friendliness). Cirque pricing reworded to the authors' own claim. Adoption hurdles now employees, business partners and the general public. Ribbonfarm critique matched to what it says (Christensen, not "positioning work"). NetJets "flying commercial". 2015 edition additions described as alignment, renewal and red ocean traps. Trimmed to about 1,494 words.
- Information Rules (about 45 claims): all ten chapters named; Goldilocks pricing now uses the book's "choose three versions" rule, with the avoid-extremes reasoning credited to the Simonson and Tversky research the book cites. Rights management overstatements cut. Seven lock-in types added. Four network strategies named. ## Confirmed and worth noting
- Business Model Generation: Wiley, July 2010. 470 co-creators from 45 countries. The definition quote is verbatim from the publisher preview. Nine blocks, five patterns, six design techniques, five process phases confirmed. Pattern examples (Lulu.com, P&G Connect + Develop, GSK, Google, Wii, Skype) confirmed against the book's pages.
- Blue Ocean Strategy: Harvard Business School Press, 2005; expanded edition Harvard Business Review Press, January 2015. Research base: 150 strategic moves over more than 100 years in 30 industries. Six-paths quote verbatim (HBS Working Knowledge excerpt). Four actions, three noncustomer tiers, strategic sequence, four hurdles, fair process's three Es, and the good-curve traits all confirmed from the authors' own excerpts.

Shapiro was Deputy Assistant Attorney General for economics at the Justice Department, 1995 to 1996; Varian joined Google in 2002 and became its chief economist.

## Process notes for future batches
- Famous examples from the general literature (VHS vs. Betamax) get attached to books that never use them. Check each example appears in the book itself.
- Watch for details from one period of a career being moved to another (Bratton's Transit Police and NYPD years).
- Ideas from an author's later article are not the book's; attribute or replace them.
- Summary sites are usually right on which examples a book uses but loose on the details.
