# Batch 1 verification notes, ranks 2 to 4 (2026-11-23)

Three separate review agents, one per book; none saw the drafting. About 94 factual claims checked. All three rated high confidence and set to verified.

An unsourced "later critics" line on mobile chips rewritten as a plain statement that Intel later lost the smartphone chip market.

## Confirmed and worth noting
- Playing to Win: Harvard Business Review Press, 2013. Lafley CEO 2000 to 2009, returned May 2013 (P&G SEC filing). The five choices, the strategy logic flow (industry structure, customer value, relative position, competitive reaction), P&G's five core capabilities, the Gillette example and the seven-step "what would have to be true" process all match the book.
- Zero to One: Crown, September 2014. Grew from Masters' notes on Thiel's Stanford CS183 class (spring 2012). Both quotes verbatim. Four monopoly traits, last-mover idea, four outlooks on the future, the power law rule and all seven questions match.
- Only the Paranoid Survive: first edition subtitle "How to Exploit the Crisis Points That Challenge Every Company and Career", Currency Doubleday, 1996. Moore exchange (mid-1985) quoted as in the book; memory exit took about three years. Pentium facts: replace-on-request December 20, 1994; $475 million charge January 1995. Section headings (Helpful Cassandras, Strategic Dissonance, Let Chaos Reign, Rein in Chaos, Traversing the Valley of Death) are real.

## Open items, resolved the same day
Joe has no print copies, so each open item was either confirmed from another excerpt or cut.
- Playing to Win: the claim that P&G weighed buying a skin care business or launching a new line for Olay rested only on Shortform; cut. The "close observation rather than surveys" gloss on customer analysis had no source; cut.
- Zero to One: the Facebook-at-Harvard start-small example confirmed by the book passage "Paradoxically, then, network effects businesses must start with especially small markets," which goes on to say Facebook started with just Harvard students (Goodreads quote page, added to sources). Clean-energy line reworded to "his case study of companies that came up short on these questions," dropping wording only secondary summaries gave.
- Only the Paranoid Survive: the motto is confirmed as Grove's line in the book but not its location, so "in the preface" became "his best-known motto." The Pentium lesson now follows Burgelman and Grove's own account (via Stanford GSB, 1996): Intel Inside had changed the rules, and the market expected Intel to behave like a consumer products company. The unsourced smartphone line was cut.
- Library of Congress records not pulled; years match publisher and catalog records.
