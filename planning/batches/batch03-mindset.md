# Batch 3: Mindset for Builders (theme 1), v01

Status: **draft for Joe's review**. 68 candidates scored, 35 picks, 5 alternates, 1 routed out after scoring. Six books routed here from Batches 1 and 2 carry their scores over unchanged.

## How to review
Mark each pick **approve**, **veto** or **swap**. Anything you don't mark counts as approved. Three calls are flagged for you below (*The 7 Habits* placement, the two contested studies at the top, and the era skew).

## Method
- **Final score = average of the rubric score** (Influence 25%, Durability 20%, Distinct idea 15%, Practical value 15%, AI-era interest 15%, Readability 10%) **and the panel score** (mean of six persona scores, 1 to 5).
- **The long list comes from** four public lists, Claude's own nominations from the canon, books routed from Batches 1 and 2, and 3 to 5 wildcards from each persona.
- **Debate decides** books near the cutoff, author caps, overlaps, theme placement and balance. Each book appears in one theme only.
- **The business-angle test** applies to every book in this theme: the Mindset Coach has to show it changes how people perform at work, lead or build. Books that failed it (*The Power of Now*, *12 Rules for Life*) are cut on that ground, not on score alone.
- **Honesty note:** the personas and all scores are Claude's judgment, not data. The verification step later checks that each book's content can be confirmed from outside sources.
- **What was different this time:** the public self-improvement lists are long on inspiration and short on evidence, so the Skeptic and the Professor did more of the work. Five of their six wildcards (*Peak*, *The Scout Mindset*, *Black Box Thinking*, *Emotional Intelligence*, *Learned Optimism*) made the list. The Mindset Coach, shut out in two batches, placed 9 of 10 here.

## Flagged for Joe
1. ***The 7 Habits* lives here, not in Habits, Focus and Performance.** The title says habits, but the book is about character and paradigms: be proactive, begin with the end in mind, the inside-out approach. The panel kept it here 5 to 1 (the Operator wanted it next to *Atomic Habits*). If you'd rather it anchor the Habits theme, *The Obstacle Is the Way* is the first alternate.
2. **Contested research near the top.** *Mindset* (2nd) and *Grit* (15th) are both flagged by the Skeptic: a 2018 meta-analysis (Sisk et al.) found small average effects for growth-mindset interventions, and a 2017 meta-analysis (Crede et al.) found grit adds little beyond conscientiousness. The panel kept both because they shaped how a generation of managers talk about talent, and the critiques sections will say so plainly. **Decided by Joe (2026-10-17):** *Flow* ranks 1st, siding with the Skeptic, and *Mindset* moves to 2nd. Ranks are Joe's call; scores are unchanged.
3. **Era skew.** 22 of 35 picks are from 2010 on, the most modern batch yet, because the modern evidence-led books (*Peak*, *The Scout Mindset*, *Black Box Thinking*) beat the older positive-thinking canon. Accept it, or ask the panel to swap a modern pick for *Self-Reliance* (1841, an alternate) or another pre-1980 classic.

## Debates
1. ***The Infinite Game* vs. *Finite and Infinite Games*.** Batch 1 left this to this batch. The Professor argued Carse is the original and the deeper book; everyone else said Sinek's is the one that applies the idea to business, which is the test for this theme. The vote went 5 to 1 for Sinek, and Carse becomes an alternate.
2. **How many Stoics.** *Letters from a Stoic*, *Meditations* and *The Obstacle Is the Way* all scored above the cutoff. The Reader's Advocate argued Holiday's book is the easiest way in. The Professor and the Skeptic answered that the site should summarize the sources, and that Seneca (on time, anger and work) and Marcus (on self-command under pressure) don't overlap much. The vote went 4 to 2 for the two primary sources. *The Obstacle Is the Way* becomes the first alternate.
3. **The deliberate-practice cluster.** *Peak*, *Talent Is Overrated*, *The Talent Code*, *Outliers*, and both books called *Mastery* compete for one idea. *Peak* is in because Ericsson is the researcher behind it and the book corrects the popular "10,000 hours" reading that *Outliers* spread. Leonard's *Mastery* is in as the short, practical book on the plateau. *Range* stays as the counterargument to early specialization. *Outliers* becomes an alternate; Colvin, Coyle and Greene are cut as overlaps.
4. ***Who Moved My Cheese?* at 31st.** The Professor and the Skeptic scored it 1: a 94-page fable with no evidence behind it. The Modernist argued it's the sharpest AI-era test on the list, since it's the book managers hand out when jobs change, and a summary that tags its ideas honestly is more useful than leaving it out. The Reader's Advocate agreed. Business fables are in scope. It stays, 4 to 2.
5. ***Four Thousand Weeks* placement.** The Modernist nominated it here. The panel agreed it's best read as an argument with the productivity canon, so it moves to Habits, Focus and Performance with its 3.74 score.
6. **The last two slots.** *Do Hard Things* (3.23) and *The Courage to Be Disliked* (3.12) against *Resilience* (Southwick and Charney, 3.19) and *Self-Reliance* (3.19). The Skeptic argued for *Do Hard Things* as the evidence-based answer to the toughness books it replaces (*Can't Hurt Me*, *Unbeatable Mind*). The Reader's Advocate argued *The Courage to Be Disliked* is the only Adlerian book on the list, a Japanese voice, and a global bestseller with real business readership. Both carried 4 to 2, which shows the debate can overrule the scores.
7. **Author caps.** Cal Newport uses one slot here; *Deep Work* and *Digital Minimalism* will compete for his second in Habits. Adam Grant uses one slot with *Originals*; *Option B* is cut, leaving room for *Think Again* or *Give and Take* elsewhere. Brene Brown gets one slot (*Daring Greatly*; *The Gifts of Imperfection* is cut as an overlap). Ryan Holiday places none, with *The Obstacle Is the Way* as an alternate.
8. **The business-angle test.** *The Power of Now*, *12 Rules for Life*, *The Four Agreements* and *The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F\*ck* are widely read but failed the Mindset Coach's own case for work impact. *Think and Grow Rich* (1937) has the reach but the Skeptic showed its claims about Hill's sources can't be confirmed, which fails the verification filter in spirit. All are cut.
9. ***Man's Search for Meaning* and the business-angle test.** The Operator called it a life book, not a business book. The Mindset Coach answered that leaders cite it on purpose at work more than almost any other book, and that its ideas transfer directly to leading through hard change. The panel agreed 5 to 1, and it ranks 3rd.
10. ***Inner Excellence* (Jim Murphy), nominated by Joe.** It wasn't on the public lists or the first long list. Murphy, a former Chicago Cubs minor-leaguer turned performance coach, first published it in 2009, revised it in 2020, and saw it reissued by a major publisher in 2025; his publisher bills him as a New York Times bestselling author. It scored 3.05 (rubric 3.10, panel 3.00). The Mindset Coach scored it 5 and argued it is the most complete modern training program for the inner game, built from two decades of work and interviews with top athletes and leaders. The Reader's Advocate backed it as practical and readable. The Professor and the Skeptic answered that it blends Stoicism, Eastern practice, psychology and some neuro-linguistic programming without much evidence of its own, and the Operator and the Modernist said *The Inner Game of Tennis* already holds the coaching-the-mind slot with the original idea. The vote went 4 to 2 against a pick. It takes *Self-Reliance*'s place as an alternate. Joe can still overrule, as he did for *Flow*.
