# Batch 4: Management, Teams and Culture (theme 6), v01

Status: **approved by Joe (2026-11-22)** with one change: *Work Rules!* replaces *The Practice of Management* (flag 1). Originally drafted with 68 candidates scored, 30 picks, 5 alternates, 2 routed to other themes. Eleven books were routed here from Batches 1 to 3; the six already scored in Batch 1 or 2 carry their scores over unchanged.

## How to review
Mark each pick **approve**, **veto** or **swap**. Anything you don't mark counts as approved. Three calls are flagged for you below (Drucker's last slot, *The Principles of Scientific Management*, and the AI-and-work books that lost).

## Method
- **Final score = average of the rubric score** (Influence 25%, Durability 20%, Distinct idea 15%, Practical value 15%, AI-era interest 15%, Readability 10%) **and the panel score** (mean of six persona scores, 1 to 5).
- **The long list comes from** six public lists, Claude's own nominations from the canon, books routed from Batches 1 to 3, and 3 to 5 wildcards from each persona.
- **Debate decides** books near the cutoff, author caps, overlaps, theme placement and balance. Each book appears in one theme only.
- **The theme's boundary with Leadership:** this theme is about running the work and the group (managing people, building teams, shaping culture, designing the organization). Books about the leader's own character, vision or change agenda go to Leadership.
- **Lead persona:** the Operator, for the honorable-mentions specialist lane.
- **Honesty note:** the personas and all scores are Claude's judgment, not data. The verification step later checks that each book's content can be confirmed from outside sources.
- **What was different this time:** the public management lists are crowded with leadership fables and general self-help, so the boundary rule did a lot of the work: 17 books were routed out before scoring. The Professor had the best batch of any persona so far (4 of 5 wildcards placed), because the research canon on teams and motivation (McGregor, Hackman, Amabile, Nonaka) is older and stronger than the popular lists suggest.

## Flagged for Joe
1. **Drucker's last slot. Decided by Joe (2026-11-22): saved for *The Effective Executive* in Habits, Focus and Performance; *Work Rules!* takes the slot here and *The Practice of Management* becomes an alternate.** Original flag: *The Practice of Management* (8th) uses Drucker's second and final slot, after *Innovation and Entrepreneurship* in Batch 2. That means *The Effective Executive* cannot go into Habits, Focus and Performance, where it was the expected Drucker pick. The panel's case: *Practice* is the founding text of the whole management field (management by objectives, "the purpose of a business is to create a customer"), and this is the theme it founded. The alternative is to leave *Practice* out here and save the slot for *The Effective Executive*; the 31st-ranked book (*Work Rules!*) would take its place.
2. ***The Principles of Scientific Management* lifted from 40th** (debate 6). Taylor scored a 1 from the Mindset Coach and 2s from the Operator and the Reader's Advocate. It's in because algorithmic management is Taylorism with better instruments, and the Modernist argued no book on the list is a sharper Expires-or-Transcends test. If you'd rather keep a working manager's book in that slot, *Work Rules!* is the first alternate.
3. **The AI-and-work books lost.** The Modernist's *Human + Machine* (3.44) and *Working with AI* (3.44) both missed. The panel's view: they are consulting case collections that will date fast, and every summary on the site already carries an AI-era lens. The theme has no book written about managing alongside AI. Accept it, or ask for a targeted round once a stronger candidate exists.

## Debates
1. **Where Management ends and Leadership begins.** The public lists mix the two freely. The panel applied one test: does the book teach how to run the work and the group, or how to be a leader? *Leaders Eat Last*, *Turn the Ship Around!*, *Multipliers*, *Dare to Lead*, *The First 90 Days*, *Leading Change* and *Hit Refresh* went to Leadership without scoring. *Radical Candor* and *The Coaching Habit* stay here because both are about the weekly mechanics of managing people.
2. **The Drucker slot** (flag 1). The Professor wanted *Practice*; the Operator preferred *The Effective Executive* in Habits. The Operator conceded that *Practice* is the founding text of this theme, and the vote went 5 to 1. *Managing for Results* (carried from Batch 1) is cut on the cap.
3. **The psychological-safety cluster.** *The Fearless Organization*, *The Culture Code*, *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team* and Edmondson's earlier *Teaming* all build on trust and safety. Edmondson takes one slot with the newer, more complete book; *Teaming* is cut. The Skeptic wanted *The Culture Code* out as a popular retelling of Edmondson's and Google's research. The Reader's Advocate answered that Coyle's book is the one managers actually finish, and it adds belonging and purpose to safety. It stayed, 4 to 2.
4. ***The Fifth Discipline* and *Team Topologies*: Management or Operations and Systems?** Operations claimed both. The panel kept *The Fifth Discipline* here because its five disciplines are about how groups learn (mental models, shared vision, team learning), and Operations keeps *Thinking in Systems*. *Team Topologies* stays because it's about designing teams around cognitive load, the question every manager now faces when AI agents join the work. Operations keeps *Accelerate* and *The Phoenix Project*. Both votes were 4 to 2.
5. **Self-management.** *Maverick*, *Humanocracy*, *Reinventing Organizations* and *The Future of Management* all argue for fewer bosses. Semler's book is the original practitioner account and a Brazilian voice; *Humanocracy* is Hamel's own update of *The Future of Management*, with newer cases. *Reinventing Organizations* lost to both on the Skeptic's point that several of its model companies later retreated from self-management; it becomes an alternate.
6. ***The Principles of Scientific Management* lifted from 40th** (flag 2). The Modernist argued that algorithmic scheduling, warehouse rate targets and AI productivity monitoring are Taylor's system with better instruments, so the book is the cleanest AI-era test in the theme. The Professor added that every later book on the list is partly an argument with Taylor, McGregor's above all. The Skeptic agreed, noting the critiques section must cover the doubts about Taylor's pig-iron figures. The vote went 4 to 2 (Operator and Mindset Coach against). *Work Rules!* drops to first alternate; it overlaps *No Rules Rules* as a big-tech people-practices book.
7. **The last culture slot: Schein vs. *Hard Facts*.** Tied at 3.63. The Professor argued Schein's three levels of culture (artifacts, espoused values, basic assumptions) are the definition everyone else borrows, including half the culture books above. The Skeptic argued for *Hard Facts* as the evidence-based management book the theme lacks. The panel noted *The Halo Effect* (Strategy) already carries much of that critique. Schein won 4 to 2.
8. **The first-time manager slot.** *The Making of a Manager* and *The Manager's Path* overlap. Zhuo's book scored higher and reads well for any function; Fournier's is the stronger book for engineering managers. Zhuo takes the slot and Fournier becomes an alternate.
9. **Author caps.** Marcus Buckingham uses both his slots here (*First, Break All the Rules* and *Nine Lies About Work*); the Skeptic pointed out that the second book partly corrects the first, which makes the pair worth keeping. Erin Meyer also uses two (*The Culture Map* and *No Rules Rules*). Robert Sutton takes one with *The No Asshole Rule*; his *Hard Facts* is an alternate. Patrick Lencioni takes one; *The Advantage* is cut. Adam Grant's *Give and Take* is routed to Sales, Persuasion and Negotiation to compete for his second slot with *Think Again*.
10. **Two routings after scoring.** *Give and Take* (3.73) is about reciprocity styles in professional relationships, which the panel judged a better fit for Sales, Persuasion and Negotiation. *Switch* (3.73) goes to Leadership to compete with *Leading Change* for the change-management slot. Both carry their scores.
