# Batch 2: Innovation and Technology (theme 9), v01

Status: **approved by Joe on 2026-10-17**, as drafted. 73 candidates scored, 30 picks, 5 alternates, 4 routed to other themes. Seven books routed here from Batch 1 were scored in this batch; the four that were already scored in Batch 1 carry their scores over unchanged.

## How to review
Mark each pick **approve**, **veto** or **swap**. Anything you don't mark counts as approved. Three calls are flagged for you below (Drucker's slot, *Prediction Machines*, and the non-US gap).

## Method
- **Final score = average of the rubric score** (Influence 25%, Durability 20%, Distinct idea 15%, Practical value 15%, AI-era interest 15%, Readability 10%) **and the panel score** (mean of six persona scores, 1 to 5).
- **The long list comes from** seven public lists, Claude's own nominations from the canon, books routed from Batch 1, and 3 to 5 wildcards from each persona.
- **Debate decides** books near the cutoff, author caps, overlaps, theme placement and balance. Each book appears in one theme only.
- **Honesty note:** the personas and all scores are Claude's judgment, not data. The verification step later checks that each book's content can be confirmed from outside sources.
- **What was different this time:** public innovation lists are thinner and older than the strategy lists, and they skew toward corporate innovation programs. The persona wildcards did more of the work as a result: 15 of the 30 picks came from a persona nomination, against 2 in Batch 1.

## Debates
1. **Christensen's author cap.** Four Christensen books made the long list. *The Innovator's Dilemma* (1st) and *Competing Against Luck* (6th) take his two slots. *The Innovator's Solution* scored 3.78 and becomes an alternate; *The Innovator's DNA* is cut. The Professor argued *Solution* is the more complete theory, and the Operator answered that jobs to be done is the idea people actually use.
2. ***The Mythical Man-Month*: Innovation or Operations?** The Operator nominated it as the founding book on why technology projects run late. The Modernist argued it belongs here because Brooks's law (adding people to a late project makes it later) is the sharpest AI-era test on the list: if AI agents add capacity without adding communication overhead, the law either expires or evolves. Operations agreed to give it up.
3. **The product cluster.** Five product books scored between 3.60 and 3.91. *Inspired* is in as the canonical product management book, and *Build* is in on the Operator's argument that it's the most practical book on making hardware and software products people want. *Sprint* and *Continuous Discovery Habits* went to a vote: the Modernist argued that when a prototype takes an hour, a five-day sprint ritual compresses, while weekly customer contact becomes more valuable. The vote went 4 to 2 for *Continuous Discovery Habits* (Operator and Reader's Advocate for *Sprint*), and *Sprint* is an alternate. *Escaping the Build Trap* is cut as too close to *Inspired*.
4. **One IDEO book.** *Change by Design* (Tim Brown) carries design thinking. *The Art of Innovation*, *The Ten Faces of Innovation* and *Creative Confidence* all come from the same firm and overlap it.
5. **One Brynjolfsson and McAfee book.** *The Second Machine Age* is in. *Machine, Platform, Crowd* overlaps it and *Platform Revolution* (Strategy).
6. **The open-source slot.** *The Cathedral and the Bazaar* (3.62) against *Working in Public* (3.44). The Modernist and the Professor argued Eghbal's book is the update Raymond needs, since it shows most open-source projects are run by one or two tired maintainers rather than a bazaar. The others held that Raymond's essay is the original idea and Eghbal's book reads best as a critique of it. The vote went 4 to 2 for Raymond; Eghbal becomes an alternate, and her argument belongs in the Raymond summary's critiques section.
7. **The AI books at the cutoff.** *AI Superpowers* is in as a 2018 forecast about China and Silicon Valley that can now be checked, and it adds a non-US voice. *AI Snake Oil* is in on the Skeptic's case that it's the only book on the list that teaches readers which AI claims fail. It was published in September 2024, which puts it just past the two-year line.
8. ***Hello World* vs. *The Soul of a New Machine*.** *Hello World* scored higher (3.58 to 3.52) but overlaps *Weapons of Math Destruction*. The Reader's Advocate argued Fry's book is the balanced counterpart to O'Neil's warning. The balance check decided it: *Hello World* is in, and *The Soul of a New Machine* is an alternate.
9. ***The Shock of the Old* lifted from 40th.** The Skeptic argued that Edgerton's book, which shows old technologies persisting long after the new ones are announced, is the strongest outside test of the site's own hypothesis that more ideas transcend than expire. The vote went 4 to 2 (Operator and Reader's Advocate against). It takes the last slot over *The Myths of Innovation*, which becomes an alternate.
10. **The Mindset Coach's nominees.** *Range*, *Originals* and *Think Like a Rocket Scientist* move to Mindset for Builders. *Creativity, Inc.* moves to Management, Teams and Culture, because its lessons are about running a creative culture. *Range* scored 3.88 here and carries that score with it.
